Strategy · M1

The growth bet

Hypothesis

The core belief

FinWise's biggest growth problem is **turning trial users into paying customers (the Revenue stage): trial-to-paid conversion is stuck at ~2% every month, whatever happens to traffic or feature usage**, because **trial users never reach a specific moment of real value during the premium period, so the reverse trial's loss aversion has nothing to work on.**

_Working notes:

The problem isn't getting users in the door, it's what happens after they arrive. A reverse trial runs on loss aversion, and users can only feel the loss of value they've actually experienced; if they never reach the Aha moment during premium, the downgrade costs them nothing.

Evidence: (1) only 2% of trial starts convert to paid, so 98 of every 100 sign-ups FinWise pays to acquire never pay; (2) most of the marketing budget goes to paid acquisition, yet adding more money is no longer driving meaningful growth, so the top of the funnel isn't the binding constraint; (3) one-year retention for paid customers is 40% (60% churn), so even users who pay don't stay, consistent with value not being established early.

Against the data: **Confirmed, and refined.**

Over 13 months (Oct 2023 – Oct 2024), the single biggest drop-off is Trial Started → Paid: 6,296 of 6,424 trials (98.0%) never converted. That is the Revenue stage, where users decide to pay. Trial-to-paid held between 1.87% and 2.08% in every month, and paid conversions tracked trials almost exactly (r = 0.99). Traffic is not the lever: monthly visits and trials are uncorrelated (r = 0.09).

The refinement: adoption of the Modeling feature rose from 10.78% to 58% over the period with no change in conversion (r = 0.25, not significant), so broader feature usage alone doesn't create the paying moment. The bet has to lead users to one specific value moment, not just more usage. The 2% → 3% target lies above anything observed in 13 months, so it is deliberately ambitious._
The bet

Where I focus

My first experiment would be a guided first-session onboarding for every new trial user that walks them to their first core value action on their own business data, because it attacks the constraint directly: the 98% of trial users who leave without paying, not the paid channels that are already saturated.

IF we guide every new trial user through a short first-session setup that ends with a real result on their own business data
THEN trial-to-paid conversion WILL increase from 2% to 3%
BECAUSE most trial users never experience FinWise's premium value before the trial ends, so the reverse trial's loss aversion has nothing to work on
MEASURED WITH an A/B test on new trial sign-ups over one full reverse-trial cycle; trial-to-paid conversion as the primary metric, week-1 core-action completion as the leading indicator, day-1 drop-off as the guardrail
Not doing: I'm not going after more paid acquisition or the visit-to-trial step, because traffic doesn't predict trials (r = 0.09) and the steepest loss happens after the trial starts. I'm also not tackling churn first, even though more customers churned than converted in every one of the 13 months: a user has to pay before they can be retained, and splitting focus there would pull effort from the bet above.
Activation · M2

The Aha moment

Aha moment

When value lands

**A trial user connects their real business finances and sees their own cash-flow picture (current balance, what's coming in and going out, and cash runway in days) in their first session.** This is the moment FinWise stops being software they are evaluating and becomes "my money, finally clear".

- **The action:** connect business data *and* view their own cash-flow snapshot.
- **The timeframe:** within the first session (day 0) of the reverse trial.
- **The habit that should follow:** returning to check the cash forecast at least 3 times in week 1.

**What led me here.** Across 13 months of FinWise funnel data (Oct 2023 – Oct 2024), trial-to-paid conversion held at ~2% regardless of feature adoption. Modeling adoption rose from 10.78% to 58% with no change in conversion (r = 0.25, not significant), so "use more features" is not the lever. The one behavioral signal that moves with how often people come back is data import: in months when more trial users imported their data, sessions per user were higher (r = 0.67, p = 0.013). Import is the set-up step, and the Aha is the first payoff on the other side of it. It also fits the reverse trial: users can only feel the loss of premium value they have seen on *their own* numbers.

**Stated assumption.** The dataset is monthly aggregates with no user-level retention, so this is the best-supported hypothesis, not a proven predictor. Validating it means comparing 90-day retention of trial users who reach their cash snapshot on day 0 against those who don't.
Retention · M3

The habit mechanic

Mechanic

The habit loop

Module 3 · Retention & Engagement. The engagement mechanic that builds the habit loop, with rationale and a wireframe.

**Mechanic:** Progress Bar: **"Week in the Clear"**. Each week has one clear finish line, *this week's cash picture is fully up to date*, reached in four quick checks. Completing it rewards the user with a concrete result about their own money, not points or badges.

- **Trigger:** A natural moment, the start of the working week, when owners plan which bills to pay and which invoices to chase. FinWise marks it with one Monday prompt ("Your week in the clear: 0 of 4. Two bills are due this week.") and timely bill alerts, nothing more. Over time the internal trigger takes over: *"Am I covered this week?"*
- **Action:** Checking their own cash-flow forecast by completing the week's four steps: review new transactions (already categorised; confirm or fix), check bills due in the next 7 days, check who still owes them (remind overdue clients, schedule reminders for upcoming ones), and check their cash runway.
- **Reward:** The progress ring fills step by step (goal-gradient effect), with copy that grows honestly ("Good start" → "Halfway there" → "Nearly there"). At 4/4: *"This week is clear ✓"*, a summary of the user's own numbers (money in, bills due and covered, money still owed, runway) plus a **"Since last time"** line that changes every week (e.g. "€2,750 more in than last week", "runway up 3 days"). That makes it a variable reward, grounded in real value.
- **Investment:** Every confirmed or corrected transaction, planned bill, chased invoice and alert makes next week's forecast more accurate *for this user*. Each cleared week is added to a visible history of "Clear" / "Partly clear" weeks. Optional extras deepen it further: a "what-if" scenario on next month (e.g. a client paying 30 days late) and sharing the week's clear picture with their accountant.

**Diagnosis first.** FinWise loses 60% of paying customers within a year of converting. Comparing the first six months of FinWise's 13-month funnel data (Oct 2023 – Mar 2024) with the last seven (Apr – Oct 2024) points to **value churn, not price churn**:
- Financial-modeling adoption nearly doubled, from 24.9% to 46.9% of users (+89%, p = 0.002), yet modeling adoption has never moved trial-to-paid conversion (r = 0.25, not significant).
- Average session length fell from 11.5 to 9.3 minutes (−19%; a trend, not statistically significant).
- The monthly churned count rose from 19.2 to 28.9 (+51%, p = 0.04).
- Sessions per user went up while sessions got shorter. Users touch more of the product and return more often, but each visit isn't solving a real problem.

Revenue per paying customer is constant across all 13 months and there's no billing-cycle data, so price churn can't be confirmed or ruled out. Exit surveys and churn timing against the billing cycle would settle it.

**This addresses value churn because** it turns each visit into one concrete answer: *exactly where my cash stands this week*. Breadth without depth is replaced by a weekly job that is finished, visibly, on the user's own numbers. Short visits are fine here (a fast, complete check is good design), as long as each one ends with a result. **A user about to churn would stay because** the value has become visible and cumulative: their forecast is accurate *because* of the weeks they've cleared, and leaving means giving up a picture they've built. The weekly cadence also means a trial user completes several cycles **before the paywall**, so the reverse trial's downgrade is felt as a real loss.

**Why this mechanic fits:**
- **Tied to a meaningful behavior:** the repeatable form of FinWise's Aha moment (seeing one's own cash-flow picture).
- **Matched to the natural cadence:** weekly, like small-business bills and invoices; daily would be nagging, and monthly gives too few repeats inside a trial.
- **Taps a real motivation:** progress toward a clear, finished week, which mirrors what missing a week actually feels like: losing sight of your cash.
- **Earns the return:** the reward is information the owner needs, with no guilt or urgency copy.
- **Connected to core value:** the mechanic *is* the core value, cash clarity, rather than points bolted onto a hollow loop.

**Decided against: a streak counter.** Missing a week feels like losing sight of your cash, not like breaking a streak, so a streak's loss aversion would fall flat. And visit frequency already rose while churn rose, so a streak would push a metric that isn't the problem. A social benchmark ("businesses like yours") was also ruled out: it adds context, not value, and could discourage the struggling businesses most likely to churn.

**How we'd know it works:** run it against a holdout group of new trial users. Measure week-4 return rate and churn per new paying customer, not just visits. Treat "Partly clear" weeks as the drop-off backlog.
Metrics · M4

What I measure

Leading

Predicts outcomes

North Star (Aim): the number of trial users who import financial data and reach their first modeling output. Both leading indicators draw a line back to it through the set-up and the habit that come before the decision to pay.

- **Day-0 data-connection rate (share of new trial users who import or connect their business financial data in their first session):** It's the set-up step every North Star user must pass first, and the only behavior in FinWise's data that moves with repeat usage (sessions per user, r = 0.67). Lifting it should feed both the North Star and the trial-to-paid decision. It has hit a ceiling at 41–45%, so the experiment should be a new way in (e.g. one-click bank connection with a sample-data fallback), not a tweak.
- **Week-1 return rate (share of trial users who come back to check their cash forecast 3+ times in their first week):** A user who forms the weekly check-in habit during the premium period has something to lose at the downgrade, which is exactly what the reverse trial needs before the Revenue decision. It's the metric a weekly "cash clear" mechanic is designed to move. Visit frequency alone isn't enough (it rose while session length hit a cliff), so it should be measured as completed weekly checks, not raw sessions.
Lagging

The outcome

- **Trial-to-paid conversion rate (baseline ≈ 2%: 128 of 6,424 trials over 13 months):** It's the Revenue-stage outcome where FinWise loses 98% of trials, and the ceiling to break. It can't be moved directly: it should rise only if more trial users connect their data, reach their own cash picture and return before the trial ends. That makes it the proof that the leading indicators were the right ones.- **Trial-to-paid conversion rate (baseline ≈ 2%: 128 of 6,424 trials over 13 months):** It's the Revenue-stage outcome where FinWise loses 98% of trials, and the ceiling to break. Data pattern

What it revealed

The headline metric is **trial-to-paid conversion at ~2%**, but the real pattern behind it is a **ceiling**: conversion stays at exactly 2% of trials no matter what happens during the trial. Over 13 months (Oct 2023 – Oct 2024), paid conversions equal trials × 2% (rounded) in every single month. The monthly rate moves by about ±0.06 percentage points, where random variation alone would move it by about ±0.63, and in 20,000 simulations of a random 2% rate none stayed this flat. Meanwhile, data import rose 19%, financial-modeling adoption nearly doubled and visits per user went up, and none of it moved conversion. **The product delivers the same outcome whatever trial users do, so more set-up or more feature usage won't break the ceiling.** It needs a new value moment before the paywall, and that has to be proven with **user-level** data (trial users who import and return vs those who don't), because monthly totals can't show what drives a rate that never moves.

_Metric patterns: Conversion, **Ceiling** (1.87–2.08% in all 13 months, no trend: r = 0.22, p = 0.48). Modeling usage, **doesn't fit cleanly**: strong growth with volatility (24.9% → 46.9% between the first and second half, p = 0.002), plus a −38% drop in Oct 2024 (58.1% → 36.0%) that could be the start of a cliff and needs watching. Import usage, **Ceiling**: after a Jan–Mar 2024 dip (~31%), it plateaued at 41–45% from Jul to Oct 2024, so more than half of users never import. Session duration, **Cliff (Jul 2024)**: steady at 11–14 min from Dec 2023 to Jun 2024, then a sharp drop to 7.0 min in Jul 2024 (−42%) that held (Jul–Oct average 7.3 vs 12.8 before). Sessions/user, **Ceiling (tentative)**: down to 3 in Jan 2024, back up to its earlier high of 8–9 from Jul to Oct 2024._

_Correlation test: **Leading 1 (data import, measured as Import Data %) vs trial-to-paid: no correlation**, r = −0.05 (p = 0.87), and r = −0.20 (p = 0.54) when import leads by one month. Leading 2's proxy (sessions per user) was also checked: r = 0.41 (p = 0.16), not significant. **Hypothesis revised:** the problem stays at the Revenue stage, but shifts from "trial users don't reach value" to "conversion has hit a ceiling that trial engagement hasn't moved". The leading indicators below are still the right ones to steer by, but they must be tested on user-level cohorts, not monthly totals._
Experiment · M5

How I test it

Method

Test design

**Method:** A/B Test (user-level randomization, 50/50, new trial users), with the **leading indicator** as the primary metric.**Why:** A standard A/B test is enough for this hypothesis, so it doesn't need anything more complex. The change (a new onboarding flow) can be randomized per user, there are only two variants, and we need a clean causal read, not real-time optimization, so a multi-armed bandit adds nothing. There's no shared environment or market-level effect, so geo or switchback testing would only cost precision. What rules out the obvious design is **time**. Testing the lagging result directly (trial-to-paid, 2% → 3%) would need 3,826 trials per arm, about **14 months** at FinWise's current ~535 trials a month, and a long-term holdout would take about 40 months. Trial-to-paid has also been fixed at 2% in every one of the last 13 months, so it can't be the metric that responds first. The experiment therefore targets the leading indicator the strategy depends on, **day-0 data connection**, which needs about 612 users per arm (**~10 weeks**). Trial-to-paid is kept as a guardrail and a longer-range secondary read.
Hypothesis

Metric + success

If we replace the current onboarding with a guided four-screen flow (a one-question focus quiz → a one-click, read-only bank connection with a "try with sample data" fallback → the user's own cash-flow snapshot, led by the focus they chose → an optional accountant invite) for **new FinWise trial users**, we expect **more of them to connect their real business data in their first session**, as measured by the **day-0 data-connection rate**.

**Primary metric:** Day-0 data-connection rate: share of new trial users who connect or import their **real** business financial data in their first session (sample-data exploration doesn't count), target: **+8 percentage points, from ~44% to ≥ 52%**, statistically significant at p < 0.05 (MDE = 8 pts; ~612 users per arm at 80% power). Baseline proxy: Get Started import usage averaged 44.0% from Jul to Oct 2024.

_Testing **the guided four-screen onboarding flow, shipped as one package behind a feature flag** (prototype: https://onboard-my-cash.lovable.app) on **all new FinWise trial users who sign up during the enrollment window, randomized 50/50 at user level** (existing customers and invited accountants excluded). Current experience: **FinWise's existing onboarding, in which importing data is a separate "Get Started" step that about 44% of users complete (Jul–Oct 2024 average); the rest never set up their data.**_

_Predicted outcome: **day-0 data connection rises from ~44% to at least 52%**, more trial users reach their own cash-flow snapshot (the Aha) in their first session, and week-1 return visits (the secondary leading indicator: 3+ forecast checks in week one) rise with it. If successful: **roll the flow out to all new trial users via the feature flag; then test its components one at a time (quiz vs one-click connect vs sample-data fallback) to learn which drove the lift, and run the "Week in the Clear" weekly mechanic as the next experiment on week-1 return. Keep reading trial-to-paid for the enrolled cohorts to see whether the leading indicator carries through to conversion.** If unsuccessful: **read drop-off by screen to find where users stall (most likely the bank-connection screen), check how many chose the sample-data path instead, and test an alternative set-up route (e.g. accounting-software or CSV import) before questioning the Aha moment itself.**_
Guardrails

Limits + read date

- **Guardrail:** _the metric that must not degrade (and by how much)._ (1) Day-1 drop-off (trial users who never return after their first session) must not rise by more than 2 percentage points versus control: a flow that forces a bank connection must not scare users away. (2) Trial-to-paid conversion in the treatment arm must not fall below the 2% baseline. (3) No increase in onboarding-related support tickets about data access or security. If any guardrail breaks, the result is read as a Mixed Signal and investigated before shipping, even if the primary metric wins.- **Read date:** _when you call it, no peeking._ Fixed before launch: **when both arms reach 612 new trial users, about 10 weeks after launch at ~535 trials a month, and no earlier.** The secondary trial-to-paid read happens once the last enrolled cohort's reverse trial has ended. No peeking: interim dashboards are for guardrail monitoring only. Avoid launching just before the December–January dip seen in last year's data (import fell to 30.5–32.2% in Jan–Mar 2024, and sessions per user hit their low of 3 in Jan 2024), and don't run any other experiment on the same onboarding surface at the same time.- **Guardrail:** _the metric that must not degrade (and by how much)._ (1) Day-1 drop-off (trial users who never return after their first session) must not rise by more than 2 percentage points versus control: a flow that forces a bank connection must not scare users away. Monetization · M6

The model

Stage 2 · Revenue Expansion, value is proven but not monetized. FinWise has reached product-market fit ($10M ARR), and trial engagement grew strongly over 13 months: financial-modeling adoption nearly doubled (24.9% → 46.9%) and data import rose to 44%. Yet trial-to-paid conversion stayed at exactly 2% in every one of those months. Rising usage that never turns into payment points to a structural pricing decision, where the paywall sits, not the price itself.**Model:** Freemium via the existing reverse trial (premium first, then a usable free tier), because FinWise delivers value continuously (an always-current picture of a small business's cash) and that value compounds with each user's own connected data and history: users can only feel the loss of what they've used. The key trade-off: a hard paywall after a free trial would protect revenue per user, but it would end the relationship with the 98% of trial users who don't convert, and lose the connected data and history that make FinWise valuable to them. A free tier keeps them connected and convertible later, at the cost of some users staying free indefinitely.
Recommendation

The move

**Redraw the free/paid line for new trial users.** When the reverse trial ends, the **free tier** keeps the bank connection and today's cash snapshot (balance, money in and out, runway): enough value to keep their data and the relationship alive. The **paid tier** holds everything that makes the picture *ongoing*: the weekly "Week in the Clear" check and its history of cleared weeks, cash alerts, the 30-day forecast and what-if scenarios, and accountant access. A trial user who has built the weekly cash-check habit loses exactly that habit at the downgrade, so the paywall sits where users feel they need more, not where the conversion goal wants it.

Testing: new signup cohorts only, so no existing customer sees a different deal; judged on conversion **and** paid retention through the 2nd–3rd billing cycle, not a first-month spike. Because packaging changes are a one-way door, it runs after the onboarding experiment confirms more trial users connect their data, so the test isn't starved of users who ever reach the line.

**The pricing bet:** Packaging matched to value. The hypothesis: if the free/paid line sits at the moment the cash picture stops being current, conversions will cluster tightly among users who formed the weekly habit, instead of staying flat at 2% across everyone.
Model signal

What success looks like

This targets the Module 4 leading indicator **week-1 return rate** (share of trial users who check their cash forecast 3+ times in their first week). Under the current packaging, conversion doesn't respond to engagement at all (2% in all 13 months while usage rose). If the line is in the right place, trial-to-paid should rise **among habitual users specifically**: the conversion rate of users with 3+ week-1 checks should pull clearly above that of users without the habit, and above the 2% baseline. The other leading indicator, **day-0 data connection**, is protected because connecting data stays free. Guardrail: paid retention through the 2nd–3rd billing cycle must hold at or above today's level (40% one-year retention means we can't trade it for a conversion spike).
Reflection · Capstone

What I learned

Friction

The hard part

The data couldn’t answer the question I was asking. Every test I ran against trial-to-paid conversion came back flat: no indicator correlated with it, and an A/B test to move it from 2% to 3% would have needed about 14 months of trials. I solved it by first checking whether conversion could vary at all (it couldn’t). Then I moved my experiments to leading indicators I could measure in about 10 weeks, day-0 data connection and week-1 return, with the sample size, guardrails and read date fixed before launch.
Aha

The moment it clicked

FinWise’s 2% wasn’t just low, it was fixed. In all 13 months, paid customers equalled exactly 2% of trials, while modeling usage nearly doubled and imports grew. Users were getting more value and none of it turned into payment. That changed my strategy: stop pushing more engagement, and redesign where value becomes visible and where the paywall sits.
Takeaways

What stuck

Name the problem before the fix: the funnel stage, the pattern (cliff, slow leak, ceiling) and the growth stage each point to a completely different action.
Experiments live in leading indicators. Set the MDE, guardrails and read date before launch, and test the line back to the business outcome rather than assuming it.
Value has to be an action the user completes, and the reward should be a real result, not points. That’s why I chose a weekly “cash clear” progress bar and ruled out a streak.
